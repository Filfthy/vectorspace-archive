AGENT X - FACTION MISSION PRESENTATION PACKS
===========================================

PURPOSE
-------

Mission mechanics are shared. The player-facing contract must not be.

Every procedural mission template should have one or more FACTION PRESENTATION
PACKS. A presentation pack controls how the same mechanical job appears on the
contract board and how the faction reacts afterwards.

The board advert tells the player what the client thinks the job is.
The mission sections tell the engine what the job actually does.
The routine completion message is normally system-generated and impersonal.
A named-contact follow-up is optional and reserved for something worth saying.

This separation is important. A QuickBite job and a Red Jackal job may execute
exactly the same section chain, but they should not read like reskins of the same
paragraph.

-----------------------

VOX                          Mara Venn

PRESENTATION PACK DATA SHAPE
----------------------------

Recommended data shape:

{
  "presentationId": "station_delivery",
  "faction": "quickbite",

  "board": {
    "titles": [
      "Priority Order",
      "Relay Delivery",
      "Hot Run"
    ],
    "descriptions": [
      "Get {cargo} to {destination}. Customer has already paid.",
      "Take the order to {destination}. Try to keep the container attached."
    ]
  },

  "routineComplete": {
    "subjects": [
      "ORDER DELIVERED",
      "DELIVERY COMPLETE"
    ],
    "bodies": [
      "Delivery confirmed at {destination}. Payment of {credits} credits has been released.",
      "Order accepted by {destination}. Contract payment: {credits} credits."
    ]
  },

  "personalFollowUps": [
    {
      "trigger": "major_encounter",
      "chance": 0.55,
      "contact": "nyxo_malloc",
      "subjects": ["DUDE"],
      "bodies": [
        "Dude. Xenos on the approach and you still got the order there. Awesome."
      ]
    }
  ]
}

The presentation pack may also contain:
  cargo labels
  target labels
  destination aliases
  board title variants
  briefing variants
  routine failure text
  encounter acknowledgement fragments
  discretionary reward text
  contact follow-up rules

Do not put section choreography here.
Do not put faction prose in the mechanical template.


PLACEHOLDERS
------------

Useful shared placeholders:

{cargo}
{destination}
{origin}
{target}
{site}
{route}
{count}
{credits}
{rep}
{encounter}
{bonusCredits}
{faction}

Avoid generating prose by blindly combining tiny sentence fragments. Prefer a
small pool of complete authored variants. The system should choose one and fill
safe placeholders.


MAIL LAYERS
-----------

1. BOARD ADVERTISEMENT

Shown before acceptance. Faction-flavoured. Short enough to scan quickly.

2. ROUTINE COMPLETION MAIL

Usually automatic and impersonal. Confirms completion, payment and reputation.
May mention a recorded encounter in factual language.

Example:
  FROM: FREE TRADERS COMPACT
  SUBJECT: CONTRACT COMPLETE

  Delivery confirmed at Relay 10. Xeno contact on approach was recorded and
  cleared. Payment: CR 4,800. Reputation: +6.

3. FAILED / ABANDONED CONTRACT REACTION

Guaranteed personal mail from the faction's principal named contact whenever an
accepted contract fails or is voluntarily abandoned. This is not a chance-based
follow-up. The contact should be pissed off, disappointed or coldly critical of
X's performance. Voluntary abandonment is normally treated more harshly than
being destroyed while attempting the job.

Corvella stays personal, overfamiliar and flirtatious even while angry. She may call X "sweetie" or "babe", but the voice should go beyond pet names into teasing disappointment and shameless comments about X's big or dangerous drone.

Kudo has zero warmth. Failure is weakness, poor discipline or failure to finish. He stays clipped and controlled rather than bureaucratic.

Cassian remains polite and highly indirect. Even when angry he prefers implication and genteel disappointment to plainly naming the crime or instruction.

Navira treats failure as lost evidence, a ruined dataset or an unresolved question. Strange complications may interest her even when the mission result is bad.

Nyxo is the profanity-frequency exception, but nothing stronger than “shit”. “Freaking” and “shiz” are fine. A failed QuickBite run can ramble about the workplace consequences, but the mission setup itself should stay short and conversational. His odd moral rationalisations are part of the voice. Avoid policy language such as “food security”. "They knew the risks" is a rare contractor-risk joke, not a recurring fatalistic attitude.

4. OPTIONAL PERSONAL SUCCESS FOLLOW-UP

Separate mail from the named contact. Only for unusual events, excellent
performance, major bonuses, discoveries, reputation milestones or story
progression.

This is where success-side personality belongs.

DISCRETIONARY REWARD LANGUAGE
------------------------------

The reward mechanic may remain generic, but the EMAIL PRESENTING IT IS PERSONAL
and must always use the faction's canonical named contact and established voice.
Generic "Procurement has attached..." / "Stores agreed..." prose is not acceptable
for a named-contact reward message.

The shared, mission-independent reward mail packs live in `mission-templates.js` as
`AgentXMissionData.rewardMail`, keyed by faction. They currently provide authored
variants for:
  credits
  free item
  supplier discount

This data is deliberately beside the mission templates because it is presentation
layer material. Campaign code owns the actual reward transaction; the template data
owns who says it and how it sounds. Existing saved generic reward messages using the
standard reward template IDs should reconstruct through these character packs.

Corvella:
  Deliberately unprofessional, overfamiliar and flirtatious. "Sweetie" / "Babe"
  remain natural, but she also shamelessly admires X's big/dangerous drone and
  treats rewards like personal favours. She can openly acknowledge that her
  correspondence is inappropriate. Never gender Agent X.

Kudo:
  Very concise and procedural. An authorised reward means more because he wastes no words.

Cassian:
  Elaborate, cultivated and slightly theatrical. Money/equipment can become a historical,
  literary or patronage analogy without turning into fake archaic English.

Navira:
  Analytical and detached. She justifies rewards through useful output, telemetry,
  probability or operational efficiency rather than sentiment.

Nyxo:
  Clerks-style foul-mouthed Dispatch voice. He can swear heavily and treat the fact that
  Corporate/Stores/Procurement actually approved something useful as absurd in itself.


DESIGN RULE
-----------

A mission template is not complete for procedural use until it has:

1. Mechanical section data.
2. At least one faction presentation pack.
3. Board title and board description variants.
4. Routine completion/failure text.
5. Optional personal follow-up rules.
6. Encounter acknowledgement rules where appropriate.
7. Reward flavour where appropriate.

This is what turns a small number of reusable mechanics into a large contract
board rather than a list of visibly duplicated missions.


LIVE STATION-DELIVERY PRESENTATION PACK
---------------------------------------

The live `station_delivery` template uses the existing groundside pickup -> launch ->
Relay 10 / Pad 03 delivery chain. All five ordinary client factions may wrap it.

Free Traders / Corvella Pallis
  Freight, spares and merchant consignments. Corvella writes adverts and personal
  mail like an overfamiliar flirt, not a professional coordinator. Success is
  admiring; failure is teasingly disappointed; abandonment is personally affronted.
  Never gender X.

OSD / Kudo Shang
  Evidence, security stores and authorised equipment. Zero warmth. Hardline warrior-culture
  language about discipline, strength and finishing the job. Praise is minimal and clipped.

Red Jackal / Cassian Erivan-Snade
  Private cases and discreet equipment. Highly indirect criminal framing. He implies that
  ownership, function or continued survival may change without plainly stating the crime.

Helix / Navira Derris
  Instruments, prototypes and samples. Analytical and intensely curious. Strange telemetry
  may interest her more than the danger that produced it.

QuickBite / Nyxo Malloc
  Food, catering and franchise stock. Nyxo swears casually, but nothing stronger than “shit”.
  “Freaking” and “shiz” are fine. His core joke is workplace absurdity plus strange moral rationalisation for violent jobs.
  “They knew the risks” remains a rare Clerks Death-Star contractors joke about hazardous
  employment. It is not generic indifference to death.

Station xeno encounter
  Routine success mail from the organisation acknowledges the unexpected infestation.
  Named personal follow-up becomes more likely and should use encounter-specific copy.
  The encounter is still not guaranteed and should never be advertised as the contracted
  objective.

Failure / abandonment
  Both always produce a named-contact response. Abandonment is generally treated more
  harshly than destruction/failure while genuinely attempting the job.

CIVILIAN TRAFFIC MISCONDUCT
---------------------------

For any template that places the player among civilian traffic, presentation is incomplete
until it covers civilian incidents as an outcome dimension.

Use separate language for:
  accidental collision / traffic contact
  deliberate weapons fire against civilian vehicles

Deliberate fire should be treated more seriously. Successful delivery does not erase it:
settlement and reputation may be reduced or cancelled, gifts are suppressed, and the
canonical named contact sends a guaranteed conduct mail instead of the ordinary clean-job
congratulation.

If the player subsequently fails or abandons the mission, fold the civilian incident into
the same named-contact failure/abandonment mail as an additional grievance.

Character rule examples:
  Corvella: remains overfamiliar and flirtatious in form while clearly angry about the conduct; teasing disappointment is preferred to generic corporate scolding.
  Kudo: terse zero-tolerance professional language.
  Cassian: frames civilian fire as conspicuous, vulgar failure of discretion.
  Navira: clinically identifies the operator/target-classification failure.
  Nyxo: mild casual profanity and workplace logic. He may explicitly reject the contractor-risk
        argument for commuters: they did not accept a hazardous orbital contract merely by
        driving to work.

LIVE TEMPLATE PACK: HOSTILE-GROUP DESTRUCTION (2026-09-07)
----------------------------------------------------------
The pure fighter contract uses faction-specific target pools. Do not use one universal list
and then randomly claim that every organisation wants every possible group dead.

Free Traders / Corvella Pallis
  Typical targets: pirates, armed extortion crews, cargo raiders and mercenaries blocking
  a trade route. Motives are route security, retaliation and protection of upcoming
  merchant traffic. Corvella's personal mail remains overfamiliar and flirtatious even when angry.

OSD / Kudo Shang
  Typical targets: wanted pirates, armed smuggler escorts that attacked patrols, rogue
  security drones, outlaw militias and hostile xeno contacts. Motives are law enforcement,
  public safety and containment. Do not casually turn normal faction traffic into OSD
  enemies merely for variety.

Red Jackal / Cassian Erivan-Snade
  Typical targets: bounty hunters, rival syndicate fighters, hostile mercenaries and
  breakaway/betraying crews. Motives include protection, commercial competition,
  retaliation and betrayal. This is the broadest morally dubious pool, but routine Jackal
  jobs should not imply open war with OSD or every other faction.

Helix / Navira Derris
  Typical targets: rogue industrial drones, research thieves, armed scavenger raiders,
  competitor-hired mercenaries and hostile xenoforms near research operations. Motives are
  containment, proprietary security and protection of research/industrial sites.

QuickBite / Nyxo Malloc
  Typical targets: pirates/hijackers preying on couriers, rogue delivery drones and xenos
  following food traffic. The joke is that a catering firm keeps acquiring an absurd
  anti-piracy problem; QuickBite does NOT order assassinations of rival takeaway companies.
  Nyxo may swear heavily in all success/failure/abandonment responses.

The target group is already armed/hostile when the combat contract begins. Do not use the
pure destroy template as a routine way to commission attacks on obvious civilian traffic.
A later destroy-then-tractor-package variant should reuse this template's target/motive
logic but is NOT part of the current pure-destruction implementation.


LIVE TEMPLATE PACK: MARKED ASTEROID CLEARANCE (2026-09-07)
----------------------------------------------------------
The asteroid-clearance template treats the field as environment and the marked subset as
the actual job. Faction presentation therefore explains WHY these particular tracked rocks
matter rather than pretending the rest of the belt does not exist.

Free Traders / Corvella Pallis
  Shipping-lane collision hazards, mining-blast fragments and merchant approach debris.
  Motives are route reopening, convoy protection and ordinary traffic safety.

OSD / Kudo Shang
  Designated navigation hazards, exclusion-zone debris and tracked fragmentation events.
  Motives are public safety, controlled-traffic release and collision prevention.

Red Jackal / Cassian Erivan-Snade
  Obstructions on discreet routes, deliberate debris placed across commercial passages,
  and hazards compromising escape/transit corridors. Cassian treats geology as an
  inconvenient participant in commerce rather than inventing an unrelated enemy faction.

Helix / Navira Derris
  Survey hazards, extraction fragments and volatile-rich bodies threatening field equipment.
  Motives are industrial safety, measurement integrity and restoration of acceptable
  operating envelopes.

QuickBite / Nyxo Malloc
  Courier-lane fragmentation, delivery-route debris and impact hazards that have finally
  become expensive enough for Corporate/Insurance to care about. Nyxo may swear heavily;
  the humour is that a food company has somehow acquired an asteroid-demolition problem.

All five packs use the canonical success/failure/abandonment correspondence rules. There is
no invented hostile group in this template: the antagonist is the identified hazard set.
