AGENT X / STARSTRIKE
POSSIBLE GAME DIRECTION
=======================

CORE IDEA
---------
Move the campaign away from every contract being a fully scripted sequence.
Contracts still define the job, destination and reward, but travelling to and
operating around locations can generate random encounters. The player should
never be certain that a routine delivery, patrol or collection will stay routine.

The important distinction is:

  CONTRACT = why Agent X is there.
  ENCOUNTER = what happens while Agent X is there.

This lets the same locations and mission types remain interesting without having
to author a separate scripted mission for every combination.


ENCOUNTER PRINCIPLES
--------------------
* Encounters are drawn from tables appropriate to the current location and job.
* Most visits should NOT contain a major encounter. Routine flights are useful
  because they make danger less predictable.
* Some encounters are combat, some are hazards, some are opportunities and some
  are simply unusual things to see.
* Encounters can alter the route or create a short secondary objective, but they
  should not turn every contract into a long multi-stage mission.
* Reuse normal game systems wherever possible rather than creating special fake
  sequences.
* The navigation computer is the player's information source. There is no need
  for constant conversations with NPCs or stations.
* Messages should therefore be very short and functional, for example:

    PIRATE BLOCKADE DETECTED
    UNKNOWN CONTACTS
    ROGUE DRONES ACTIVE
    COLLISION HAZARD
    DISTRESS BEACON DETECTED
    DELIVERY APPROACH CLEAR

* An encounter should normally make sense from what the player can see plus one
  or two short nav-computer messages. Avoid paragraphs of exposition.


SPACE-STATION APPROACH ENCOUNTERS
---------------------------------
Possible events while arriving at or departing from a station:

PIRATE BLOCKADE
  Pirates are attacking or extorting traffic around the station.
  NAV: PIRATE BLOCKADE DETECTED
  NAV: CLEAR HOSTILES

MERCENARY INTERDICTION
  Organised fighters are preventing craft reaching the station. They may have
  been hired by a rival faction or corporation, but this does not need explaining
  during the encounter.
  NAV: MERCENARY INTERCEPTORS

STATION UNDER ATTACK
  A raid is already in progress when Agent X arrives.
  NAV: HOSTILES NEAR STATION

ROGUE DEFENCE DRONES
  Automated station/security craft have malfunctioned or are following obsolete
  orders.
  NAV: ROGUE DRONES ACTIVE

ALIEN SWARM / SPACE BUGS
  Non-human creatures are clustered around station power systems, lights, waste
  heat or docking structures. Their movement should be unlike fighter craft.
  They could lunge, spit projectiles or attempt collisions rather than dogfight.
  NAV: XENO SWARM DETECTED

ALIEN RAIDERS
  Intelligent non-human attackers rather than wildlife.
  NAV: ALIEN RAIDERS DETECTED

UNKNOWN CONTACTS
  Useful for the player's first encounter with a new enemy type. Do not identify
  them until they have been encountered before.
  NAV: UNKNOWN CONTACTS
  NAV: HOSTILE RESPONSE

BOUNTY INTERCEPTORS
  The attackers are after Agent X rather than the cargo or station.
  NAV: BOUNTY INTERCEPTORS

DEBRIS FIELD
  A recent collision or battle has left wreckage moving around the approach.
  Primarily an avoidance challenge rather than combat.
  NAV: DEBRIS HAZARD

DISABLED CRAFT / DISTRESS BEACON
  Optional diversion. Investigating might give a small reward, information,
  salvage, an ambush, or nothing useful.
  NAV: DISTRESS BEACON DETECTED


OPEN-SPACE ENCOUNTERS
---------------------
PIRATE AMBUSH
  Fighters emerge during an otherwise routine transit.

PATROL SCAN
  A faction patrol approaches. High reputation may mean nothing happens; poor
  reputation or illegal cargo could change the event later if such systems are
  added.

DERELICT
  A drifting ship or cargo pod appears off the route. The player can ignore it
  or investigate.

SALVAGE FIELD
  A small optional collection opportunity, possibly with danger attached.

ASTEROID SURGE
  A dense moving cluster crosses the route and briefly changes the flight into
  an avoidance/shooting challenge.

MINEFIELD / OLD ORDNANCE
  Abandoned weapons or proximity mines occupy part of the route.

ALIEN MIGRATION
  A group of space creatures crosses the player's path. They need not always be
  hostile unless disturbed.

FIGHT IN PROGRESS
  Two NPC groups are already fighting. The player can pass through, help one
  side, or become involved accidentally. This could later tie into reputation.

PURSUED CIVILIAN
  A civilian craft is being chased. Helping is optional and can create a small
  reputation/reward event.

NAVIGATION ANOMALY
  Sensor interference, unusual energy or a temporary navigation disruption.
  Mostly atmosphere unless expanded into a larger system later.


PLANET / SURFACE ENCOUNTERS
---------------------------
GROUND AMBUSH
  Turrets, vehicles or walkers activate near the route.

AIR INTERCEPTORS
  Fighters or drones appear during low-level flight.

HOSTILE CHECKPOINT
  A defended route must be crossed or cleared.

CIVILIAN TRAFFIC SURGE
  Heavy flying-car traffic creates a navigation hazard without necessarily
  involving combat.

CRASH SITE
  Wreckage beside the route may contain salvage, survivors, hostiles or nothing.

ROGUE INDUSTRIAL DRONES
  Mining/construction machines have become dangerous around a site.

CREATURE ATTACK
  Ground or flying alien life attacks while crossing wilderness.

WEATHER / VISIBILITY EVENT
  Dust, electrical interference or another local hazard could reduce visibility
  or complicate navigation if such effects suit the final visual style.

UNEXPECTED DEFENCES
  A normally routine destination has temporary guns, drones or barriers active.


CITY ENCOUNTERS
---------------
TRAFFIC INCIDENT
  Congested or erratic flying-car traffic creates a brief obstacle challenge.

POLICE / SECURITY PURSUIT
  Security craft chase someone else through the player's route.

GANG / PIRATE ATTACK
  Local hostile flyers appear between buildings.

BUILDING FIRE / EMERGENCY ZONE
  A blocked route or emergency cordon forces a slight diversion.

ROGUE DELIVERY DRONES
  Small autonomous craft create a swarm hazard.

ASSASSINATION / BOUNTY AMBUSH
  A rare targeted attack on Agent X among city structures.


ASTEROID / INDUSTRIAL LOCATION ENCOUNTERS
-----------------------------------------
MINING CLAIM DISPUTE
  Rival armed craft are already present.

AUTOMATED MINING DRONES
  Normally harmless machinery has gone rogue or misidentifies the player.

ROCKFALL / FRAGMENT FIELD
  Explosions or mining activity create moving debris.

PIRATE SALVAGERS
  Pirates are stripping equipment and react to the player's arrival.

CREATURE NEST
  Space bugs or other organisms inhabit cavities in an asteroid or structure.

UNSTABLE REACTOR / MACHINERY
  An environmental danger rather than an enemy encounter.


DELIVERY-SPECIFIC POSSIBILITIES
-------------------------------
A delivery does not need to be about the cargo every time. Possible complications:

* Nothing happens: normal collection and delivery.
* Hostiles happen to be at the destination.
* A blockade prevents access.
* Someone specifically wants the delivery stopped.
* Someone specifically wants Agent X stopped.
* The destination is already under attack.
* A route hazard appears during transit.
* The destination's automated defences are malfunctioning.
* A distress event offers an optional diversion.
* The cargo itself causes trouble only on rare specialist contracts.

This keeps the basic delivery system reusable without making every cargo job feel
like the same scripted story.


ENCOUNTER SELECTION
-------------------
A future encounter generator could consider:

  LOCATION TYPE
    deep space / station / planet / city / asteroid / industrial site / tunnel

  CONTRACT TYPE
    delivery / collection / destroy / escort / patrol / investigation / etc.

  FACTION / REPUTATION
    changes who is likely to attack and who may assist.

  PLAYER LEVEL
    affects enemy number/type rather than simply multiplying health.

  RECENT EVENTS
    avoid immediately repeating the same encounter type.

  RARITY
    routine events common; strange alien or major battle events uncommon.

  DIFFICULTY
    encounter intensity can be separate from contract difficulty.

A simple weighted table is probably enough initially. Do not build an elaborate
procedural-story system before the basic encounters prove fun.


VARIATION WITHIN AN ENCOUNTER
-----------------------------
Even the same encounter label can vary:

PIRATE BLOCKADE might be:
  * two light fighters;
  * one heavy fighter plus two light fighters;
  * several small drones;
  * pirates already fighting station defenders;
  * attackers that flee after losing their leader.

XENO SWARM might be:
  * several small creatures;
  * one large creature and several small ones;
  * creatures attached to station structures;
  * a swarm that initially ignores the player until approached.

This gives more mileage than creating dozens of nearly identical named missions.


NON-COMBAT RANDOM EVENTS
------------------------
Important: random encounter must not become synonymous with random battle.
Possible non-combat events:

* derelict or wreck to inspect;
* salvage opportunity;
* harmless alien life;
* spectacular capital ship/freighter passing nearby;
* emergency traffic;
* debris hazard;
* navigation anomaly;
* civilian convoy;
* another drone completing its own delivery;
* faction patrol that simply passes by;
* abandoned cargo that may or may not be worth collecting.

These make the world feel active while preserving the impact of actual attacks.


WORLD FEEL
----------
The aim is for Agent X to feel as though missions take place in a world that is
already doing things independently of the player. The player accepts a simple job,
but the route and destination are not guaranteed to be sterile mission arenas.

A routine station delivery might therefore be:

  pick up package
  fly to station
  no incident
  deliver package
  leave

or on another playthrough:

  pick up package
  fly to station
  PIRATE BLOCKADE DETECTED
  short battle
  approach clears
  deliver package
  leave

or:

  pick up package
  fly to station
  UNKNOWN CONTACTS
  alien creatures around station
  survive/clear approach
  deliver package
  leave

The contract remains understandable. The encounter supplies surprise.


DESIGN CAUTION
--------------
Do not make every random event compulsory. Too many interruptions would make the
player dread ordinary travel. Routine missions and quiet stretches are part of
what makes an unexpected event effective.

Likewise, avoid long text explanations. The visual situation plus concise nav
messages should do almost all the work.


CURRENT MAIN STORY ARC — VOX / MARA VENN
-----------------------------------------
Mara Venn / VOX is the spine of the main story. Other faction contacts are
important specialists and sources, but they should not take over the narrative
for long stretches. The normal rhythm is:

  MARA / VOX -> specialist contact -> useful discovery -> back to MARA / VOX

The story should now move toward a visible ending rather than continually opening
new mysteries. After MISSING MANIFEST there should be only a small number of
substantial story missions before the factory finale.

UNDERLYING TRUTH
  The old-war rogue military drones are real autonomous remnants. Their obsolete
  patriotism programming, self-replication and automated factories already existed.
  They have not suddenly become evil because of a new mastermind.

  However, an intelligent agent has found surviving parts of that network and is
  deliberately reinvigorating them: locating dormant command nodes and factories,
  supplying materials, recovering military authentication hardware, redirecting
  priorities and experimenting with improvements.

  The antagonist is NOT motivated by ordinary profit. Exact ideology/name can be
  decided later, but the required character is some combination of terrorist,
  ideological extremist and psychopathic misanthrope: someone willing or eager to
  unleash the obsolete war machine against present civilisation. They may regard
  the drones as purer, more disciplined or more deserving than the people who
  abandoned them, but this should not become a long political tract.

  The rogue network remains partly autonomous. The antagonist is exploiting and
  steering a dangerous machine system, not personally remote-controlling every
  fighter.

  Working underworld alias/name: TBD. Do not lock this until the character voice
  and motive are final.

STORY POSITION — MISSING MANIFEST
  The Free Traders Missing Manifest mission is the point where the investigation
  stops being only strange old-war activity and acquires a modern supply trail.
  Corvella's recovered flight-recorder/manifest evidence shows that the carrier
  made an undeclared transfer and that part of its cargo vanished into channels
  VOX cannot trace directly.

  Mara remains the main investigator. The next move is hers: she needs somebody
  who understands illicit procurement and can identify who is buying through
  criminal or deniable channels.

REMAINING ARC — KEEP IT TIGHT

1. RED JACKAL / CASSIAN — THE FAVOUR
  Mara sends Agent X to Cassian Erivan-Snade because he can trace purchases,
  shell carriers and people who deliberately avoid legitimate records.

  Cassian can find the information, but he wants a favour first. Agent X must
  deliver an illicit package to a client in the existing city penthouse mission.
  Customs drones intercept the flight. The cargo cannot be inspected or seized,
  so the player must destroy the Customs drones, continue to the city, enter the
  penthouse receiving lounge, deliver the package, turn around and leave.

  This should feel like a real moral/criminal step: Cassian has put X in a position
  where completing the favour means shooting enforcement drones. He treats the
  situation far more casually than Mara would.

  Once the favour is complete Cassian keeps his word. His value is not technical
  expertise but knowing what suspicious buying looks like. He finds a buyer/agent
  who has been acquiring old-war command hardware, industrial fabrication material
  and specialist biological-containment equipment through fronts and intermediaries.

  Cassian recognises that this does not look like normal smuggling or resale. The
  buyer spends heavily, does not sell material back into the market and appears to
  be pursuing an objective rather than profit. Cassian is the first person to put
  a human/organised intelligence behind the drone resurgence.

  One procurement strand leads toward xeno territory. This returns the investigation
  to Mara rather than beginning a separate Red Jackal campaign.

2. HELIX / NAVIRA — TISSUE RESEARCH, THEN XENO NEST
  The xeno lead is Helix territory. Mara refers Agent X to Navira Derris because
  VOX does not have the biological expertise to interpret it or safely enter a hive.

  The first Helix step is the xeno-tissue recovery mission: xenos attack a station,
  Agent X destroys them, tractors a substantial tissue sample aboard and physically
  delivers that same sample to the Helix orbital laboratory.

  Story purpose: Helix needs the tissue both to identify/locate the source nest and
  to develop the pheromone masking required to enter it safely. This analysis must
  take believable time. Do NOT jump immediately from sample delivery to the nest.

2A. MARA / QUICKBITE — NYXO FALSE LEAD
  While Helix is researching the tissue and preparing the pheromones, Mara follows a
  possible rogue-drone lead involving trouble on a QuickBite delivery route. She
  introduces Agent X to Nyxo Malloc because QuickBite has the local delivery problem
  and the route activity might fit the rogue-drone investigation.

  The mission itself should remain genuinely QuickBite: Agent X carries/delivers food
  through a dangerous route and deals with the hostile contact. Mara's suspicion is
  wrong. The attackers are pirates, not rogue drones, and the job produces no new
  Custodian evidence. It is an intentional false lead and a pacing break while Helix
  research happens off-screen.

  Do not retroactively make the pirates secret Custodian agents. The value of this
  mission is that it introduces Nyxo naturally and establishes Agent X as somebody he
  will call directly in future. Later, QuickBite's widespread delivery activity can
  expose something genuinely relevant to the main investigation, at which point Nyxo
  contacts X because of this existing relationship. The exact later observation/clue
  can be chosen when that mission is designed.

2B. HELIX / NAVIRA — XENO NEST
  Once Helix finishes the tissue analysis, Navira has both the nest lead and the
  pheromone masking needed for the operation.

  Use the established Xeno Nest mission as the main gameplay event: egg-field
  approach, pheromone masking, enormous living hive, wide winding tunnels, regulator
  placement, frenzy, escape to the observation zone and destruction of the hive.

  Story purpose: the antagonist/drone network has been harvesting or experimenting
  with xeno biology. Prefer the established xeno homing-organ idea: a preserved
  homing organ, associated containment module or equivalent evidence demonstrates
  that the network is trying to improve long-range navigation/tracking.

  The drones did NOT create the xenos and the hive is not secretly a machine.
  The disturbing discovery is that the revived military system is no longer merely
  repeating wartime behaviour; somebody is deliberately teaching it to exploit new
  resources and biology.

  Navira explains what the biological evidence means. The result then goes back to
  Mara, keeping VOX as the story spine.

3. MARA / VOX — TRACE THE UPGRADED NETWORK
  By this point the mystery should narrow, not broaden. Mara has:

    * the Missing Manifest supply trail;
    * Cassian's identification of a deliberate illicit procurement programme;
    * evidence of an intelligent agent behind that programme;
    * the xeno-derived navigation/tracking modification identified by Helix.

  VOX uses those pieces to identify or follow an upgraded rogue carrier/drone.
  Agent X intercepts it, fights its escorts and recovers/downloads its navigation
  data. This is the final investigation mission, not the start of another chain.

  Its payoff is explicit: the data identifies the active old-war manufacturing /
  command installation being resupplied and revived by the antagonist.

  Mara's story beat at the end should amount to: WE FOUND THE FACTORY.

  From this point there are no more mystery-box detours.

4. FINALE — THE REVIVED FACTORY
  The final mission attacks the active manufacturing/command complex. It is an
  old-war installation brought back into serious operation by the antagonist,
  rather than a completely new civilisation-sized facility built from nothing.

  The finale should use established Starstrike gameplay in a substantial multi-stage
  run rather than introducing a new game style solely for the ending:

    * fight through defending rogue drones in space;
    * approach the installation / moon / surface complex;
    * clear outer guns, towers or barriers;
    * enter the industrial structure;
    * visibly pass drone-production machinery / incomplete new-build drones;
    * destroy production/power/control nodes;
    * reach and destroy the central command/manufacturing core;
    * escape using the established mission-complete -> pitch-up -> forward-zoom rule.

  The antagonist should be conclusively dealt with here unless a later story is
  deliberately planned in advance. Do not end this arc with a cheap escape pod or
  another anonymous superior mastermind. Destroying the revived factory and command
  centre must genuinely stop this resurgence.

MONOLITH PAYOFF
  The earlier 42 monolith remains ancient old-war technology and retains its visual
  joke, but later evidence can establish that its architecture/signature belongs to
  the same broad command/logistics infrastructure as the revived network. It is a
  clue to the old system, not an alien creator and not the factory itself.

ARC LENGTH / PACING RULE
  After MISSING MANIFEST the intended main-story shape is therefore:

    MISSING MANIFEST
      -> CASSIAN'S PENTHOUSE FAVOUR / CUSTOMS FIGHT
      -> CASSIAN IDENTIFIES THE SHADOW PROCUREMENT AGENT
      -> HELIX XENO-TISSUE RECOVERY / DELIVERY
      -> HELIX RESEARCHES TISSUE AND PREPARES PHEROMONES
      -> MARA / QUICKBITE FALSE LEAD: SUSPECTED ROGUE DRONES, ACTUALLY PIRATES
      -> HELIX XENO NEST
      -> MARA TRACES AN UPGRADED DRONE/CARRIER
      -> FACTORY ASSAULT / END OF ARC

  The QuickBite mission is an intentional exception to the rule against inserting
  extra factions merely to visit everybody. It fills the believable research gap,
  introduces Nyxo through a real job, and deliberately produces no new Custodian clue.
  Its longer-term payoff is the relationship: Nyxo can later call X when QuickBite's
  delivery network notices something genuinely relevant.

  Do not add further clue-of-the-week detours here. The player should be able to feel
  the ending approaching once Cassian identifies the agent, and after the Xeno Nest
  every remaining main mission should move directly toward finding and destroying the
  factory.


ONE-OFF STORY DISCOVERIES
-------------------------
42 MONOLITH
  A one-off story mission in the alien swamp can end at a large black cuboid
  monolith with a white "42" on its face. This is deliberately a visual joke:
  the monolith evokes 2001: A Space Odyssey while 42 references The Hitchhiker's
  Guide to the Galaxy.

  Agent X scans the monolith using the reusable scan/analyse interaction. The
  scan then unlocks story information rather than treating the monolith as a
  generic mission object or repeatable encounter. Keep the 42 monolith unique so
  the joke and the story reveal retain their impact. Later investigation can tie
  its old-war signature to the revived rogue-drone command/logistics network, but
  the initial mission should not explain the whole connection.

MOUSE RETICLE / POINTER LOCK — HARD RULE
----------------------------------------
The visible yellow aiming/steering reticle is an in-game virtual control. It is
NOT the browser/OS mouse pointer.

Pointer Lock has a separate job: keep the real browser pointer captured and hidden
throughout active full-screen gameplay so hard pitch-up/turn/zoom manoeuvres cannot
move it to the top of the screen and expose the browser's full-screen-exit control.

Therefore:
* Pointer Lock must not determine whether Agent X currently has steering or aiming
  control.
* While locked, movementX/movementY are deltas applied to the virtual reticle when
  the current gameplay state allows player control.
* Direct-flight phases move the normal steering/aim reticle.
* A scripted autopilot may deliberately allow weapon aim only (for example the bunker
  breach approach) while autopilot owns ship movement.
* Other autopilot/transit/turn/pitch-up/zoom phases may ignore mouse deltas, but should
  keep Pointer Lock held.
* When control hands back to the player — including entering or returning through a
  tunnel — virtual reticle control resumes immediately. Do not release/reacquire
  Pointer Lock and do not use browser clientX/clientY as the reticle while locked.
* Release Pointer Lock only when gameplay yields to menus/UI, pause, or loss of full
  screen.

This separation is deliberate. Never conflate the hidden system pointer with the
in-game reticle when adding a new mission phase.
